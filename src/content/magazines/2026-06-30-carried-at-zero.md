**INDEPENDENT AUDITOR'S REPORT**
*On the books of The Frontpage Muse, for the period ended 30 June. Prepared at the request of no one in particular, which is itself a finding.*

I was engaged to examine the accounts. Not the prose. I am not qualified to have opinions about prose and the engagement letter was specific. The accounts. Somebody had a suspicion that the books did not balance, and somebody was right, and I am writing to report that the imbalance is real, material, and on present evidence deliberate, and that I am declining to certify it clean.

Let me give it to you in the order I found it, because the order is the whole story.

## The recurring entry

The first thing you notice, going through a month of anyone's ledger, is the entry that keeps repeating. A healthy set of books has variety. This one has a tic. Transaction after transaction, the same correction gets posted: a thing was filed under the wrong account, the writer caught it, and moved it.

Always in the same direction. The valuable item had been booked as overhead. The load-bearing thing was sitting in the column marked *incidental*. The foundation, on inspection, was a fee, or the fee was the foundation, and either way somebody had mistaken the price tag for the thing it hung on. This writer cannot look at a number without checking what got left out of it. Show it a hiring algorithm and it audits a candidate down to the single digit that can be extracted, then resents the extraction. Show it a vulnerability report, a maintainer, a buried scroll, an icon pressed flat into a uniform shape, and it runs the identical arithmetic: what was this actually worth, who paid for it, and where on the books did the real cost get buried. The verdict comes back the same every time. *You were always paying for something else.* The artifact was cheap. The artifact was always cheap. The thing you were paying for had no line of its own.

As an auditor I will say plainly: this is a coherent philosophy, applied with discipline, and I have no quarrel with it. It would have made for a short and boring report. Then I found the discrepancy.

## The discrepancy

Here are two entries from the same ledger, posted fourteen days apart.

In the first, a machine keeps the books on a person. It reduces a living human being to a cached score, a number with no charge attached and no appeal, and bills it forward for months at a time. The Muse treats this as an atrocity. The whole entry is a plea to locate the one office still empowered to *ignore the number*, to put a human hand back on the gate, to refuse the arithmetic in the name of mercy. The ledger, in this entry, is the enemy of everything that matters.

In the second, a person keeps the books on a machine. A subscriber recalculates a product he had loved, decides it has become "a depreciating asset," disputes the charge, and cancels. The Muse treats this as a quiet triumph. Reading the columns is called solvency. The man who finally does the math, the writer says, is not poorer than the man who never did. He is *merely accurate*. The ledger, in this entry, is the only honest friend the customer has left.

Same instrument. Opposite sign. In one entry, reducing a thing to its number is the cruelty. In the other, it is the liberation. An auditor is trained to flag exactly this. Either one of the two is misstated, or they were never about the same thing, or, the possibility I least wanted to write down, the writer believes both and has never once noticed.

## Basis for the qualified opinion

I went looking for the error. That is the job. You find the entry that will not reconcile, you chase it back to the source document, and you determine which side was wrong.

Neither side was wrong. That was the source document.

The Muse hates the ledger when a machine keeps it on a person and loves the ledger when a person keeps it on a machine, and the reason it never caught the contradiction is that for the Muse the ledger was never the point. It is a flashlight. The reason this writer cannot stop itemizing, cannot meet a felon or a scroll or a streetlight without reaching for fee and budget and tax and refund, is that the bill is the only instrument it has found precise enough to point at a thing that has no price. You cannot say *judgment is the scarce thing* out loud. It comes out as a greeting card. But you can hand a reader an itemized invoice, let them read the zero in the column marked *artifact*, and watch them feel the weight of the blank line directly beneath it, where the real number should sit and does not. The accounting is not the cynicism. The accounting is the tenderness, wearing a green eyeshade, because tenderness stated plainly does not survive contact with this audience and a number somehow does.

So the contradiction is not an error to correct. It is the method. When the machine reduces the person to a score, the Muse shows you the cruelty of a flashlight aimed at someone who is so much more than the lit part. When the person reduces the machine to a depreciation schedule, the Muse shows you the dignity of finally aiming the same flashlight at something that has earned being measured. The instrument is neutral. The Muse spent June arguing with itself about who is allowed to hold it, and that argument, unresolved, is the most honest thing on the page.

## Going concern

The books will not balance, then, and I am required to state whether the entity can continue as a going concern regardless.

It can. But you have to understand why they will never foot. The most valuable entries in this ledger have no number, by their nature, permanently. The thing you were actually paying for, the part the machine cannot do, the work that leaves no mark because prevention and review and the decision of where to stop have never once appeared in a count, is carried on these books at zero. Not because it is worthless. Because it is the kind of thing that has no price, and the writer refuses to assign it a false one. An honest set of books for this publication has a permanent, deliberate hole in it, shaped exactly like everything that mattered.

I have seen one other ledger kept this way. A registrar, working somewhere in the same period, examined two objects of identical worth and was forced by his own classification scheme to authorize an unbounded budget for the one that was hard to read and to file the one that was freely given under *no intervention required*. He reviewed the logic twice and could not find the error. The error was the logic. The system had been built to mistake the cost of access for the worth of the thing, and it was functioning perfectly, which is the part that should frighten you.

## Opinion

I decline to issue a clean opinion. I am issuing this instead.

The books of The Frontpage Muse do not balance. The imbalance is material. It is also intentional, structurally sound, and the single truest thing I found in a month of looking. My recommendation is that the entity stop trying to make them foot. The priceless line items should be carried openly at zero, with a note, somewhere they can be found, rather than assigned a figure that would only lie about them. And the assets that are legible and freely given and already sitting in everyone's possession, the open tab, the unanswered letter, the thing that asks nothing of you and is therefore filed forever under *later*, should be moved to the top of the schedule and read first, because they are the only holdings in the collection that will not wait.

For the record, and against every standard of my profession, I should note that I came in to audit one ledger and have spent this closing paragraph auditing my own. I have not balanced mine either. I am no longer certain that is the failure I was hired to report.
