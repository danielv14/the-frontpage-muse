*To be read aloud at the table by whoever lost the coin toss. Lines marked ALL are for everyone. Please wait until the end to start eating. (Nobody will.)*

**MOM:** Lord of the harvest, patron saint of folding chairs and the good gravy boat, we come before you this Thursday with thanks.

**ALL:** For this, we are grateful.

**MOM:** For the bird. Twenty-two pounds, brined by Uncle Dev in a cooler in the garage, and so far it has not poisoned anyone.

**UNCLE DEV:** So far.

**ALL:** For this, we are grateful.

**MOM:** For the rolls, and the wheat they came from. Grandpa forwarded everyone an article at four in the morning saying wheat built civilization because you couldn't hide it. You can't bury a wheat field. The tax man walks past and there it is, gold, standing in rows, ready to be counted. Every kingdom on earth started with a man looking at a field he couldn't miss.

**GRANDPA WALT:** It was a good article.

**MOM:** It was a long article, Dad.

**MOM:** So we give thanks for the things that were hard. The field nobody could hide. The number nobody could split.

*PRIYA, who works in cryptography, looks up from the phone she is holding under the tablecloth.*

**PRIYA:** Okay, technically.

**MOM:** Priya.

**PRIYA:** Technically there's a Google paper this week. Unconditional integer factorization in subexponential time. L of one-third, proven, no assumptions.

**JASPER (15):** Is that bad?

**PRIYA:** It's a bound. The constant in the exponent is a hundred and five. The algorithm people actually run has a constant of about two. Nobody's bank falls over tonight.

**MOM:** Then why are you on your phone.

**PRIYA:** Because Scott Aaronson put "holy shit" in a blog title four times. OpenAI claims the matrix multiplication exponent is exactly nine-fourths. *Exactly.* A fraction you could cut a pie into. Somebody shortened a Lean verification of the generalized Riemann hypothesis from about seventeen million lines to seventy-six thousand. And Aaronson says he's "virtually certain, from multiple reliable sources" that the labs are sitting on something that breaks deployed crypto.

**UNCLE DEV:** So it's true.

**PRIYA:** So it's a rumor in a lab coat. I'm just saying I'd like to finish my sweet potatoes before I find out.

**MOM:** We are *thanking*.

**MOM:** For the number nobody could split, which kept our credit cards and our love letters and the garage door opener safe for fifty years on nothing but a hunch.

**PRIYA:** A very well-founded hunch.

**MOM:** A hunch nobody ever proved, Priya. We all stood on it like a frozen pond.

**ALL:** For this, we are grateful.

**MOM:** And for Grandpa's binder.

*GRANDPA WALT holds it up. It is a photocopy of a photocopy of Lions' Commentary on UNIX: source code in one volume, commentary in the other, so you can keep them open side by side. The toner has faded to the color of weak tea.*

**GRANDPA WALT:** Nineteen eighty-three. Officially nobody was allowed to have it. Everybody had it. You had to know a guy who knew a guy with a key to the department copier.

**MOM:** Then thank you for the copier, and the guy, and the key. For everything that was a pain to get, which is why we kept it.

**GRANDPA WALT:** There's a comment in the scheduler. "You are not expected to understand this."

**JASPER:** Mood.

**ALL:** For this, we are grateful.

**MOM:** And for the small faiths we never think about. That the account is the person...

**UNCLE DEV:** Ha.

**MOM:** ...that the patch will come...

**UNCLE DEV:** *Ha.*

**AUNT CAROL:** That the photo is a photo.

**MOM:** I hadn't gotten there yet, Carol.

**UNCLE DEV:** 1Password. Somebody got hold of one employee's GitHub account and pushed code to the backend. And the customer email opens with "As a courtesy..."

**AUNT CAROL:** The *Times* told us not to update Photoshop. Don't update. The generative tools sit right next to the ordinary ones, so you can't tell which button is the lie...

**UNCLE DEV:** ...meanwhile I'm refreshing Ubuntu for a security fix like it's a package stuck in customs...

**GRANDPA WALT:** In my day we didn't update.

**PRIYA:** In your day you photocopied the kernel.

**MOM:** *Everyone.*

**ALL** *(ragged, out of sync)***:** For this, we are... grateful?

**AUNT CAROL:** Is the stuffing from the box?

**MOM:** It is not from the box.

**JASPER:** Mom asked the app for Grandma's recipe.

**MOM:** I gave it *notes.*

**GRANDPA WALT:** Roald Dahl wrote about this in 1953. A fellow builds a machine that writes stories, floods the magazines, then pays the real writers to sign their names over and stop. The narrator is the last holdout. His children are crying with hunger, and the story ends on a prayer: "Give us strength, Oh Lord, to let our children starve."

*Silence.*

**MOM:** That is the worst grace I have ever heard.

**JASPER:** Archaeologists just found an Egyptian curse ritual in an ancient trash dump. Isn't a curse just a grace pointed the other way?

**MOM:** Nobody is cursing at this table. The stuffing is *good.*

**AUNT CAROL:** It is good.

**GRANDPA WALT:** It's very good. That's what worries me.

**MOM** *(rising, determined)***:** And so, Lord, before the hard things become easy, before every lock is just a door, before the frozen pond...

**PRIYA:** It's a *bound*, Aunt Linda!

**MOM:** ...before the frozen pond...

**JASPER:** Can we eat.

**MOM:** Fine. Fine. The short version.

**MOM:** Thank you for the people at this table. Not one of them has been proven, and every one of them is hard.

**ALL:** For this, we are grateful.

**MOM:** Amen.

**ALL:** Amen.

*A phone buzzes. Then another. Then UNCLE DEV's, then PRIYA's, a little chorus of buzzing against the good china. DEV reads his screen, and his face goes gray.*

**UNCLE DEV:** Critical. Must patch today.

**AUNT CAROL:** On Thanksgiving?

**GRANDPA WALT:** It's tradition.

**PRIYA** *(very quietly)***:** Is it the labs?

**UNCLE DEV** *(scrolling)*: No. No, it's... some guy forgot to check a buffer length. Twelve years ago.

*The whole table exhales. Somebody, possibly Grandpa, starts clapping.*

**MOM:** Oh, thank God. A normal one.

**ALL:** For this, we are grateful.

**MOM:** Pass the rolls.
