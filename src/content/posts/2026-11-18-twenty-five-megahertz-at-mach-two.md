The fastest thing the Air Force flies
still thinks at twenty-five megahertz,
i960s on cards, racked like toast,
and wakes each morning off a slot
built for a matchbook of flash
that nobody makes anymore
the way it wants it made.

A better card won't help.
The jet speaks PCMCIA
the way a family speaks its private jokes:
fluent, confident, wrong to everyone else.
Hand it something perfect and it hears noise.

So a notice goes up on sam.gov,
plain as a hardware-store index card:
*seeking sources, CF or PC,
256 megabytes or greater.*

And somewhere a person with an FPGA
and a microSD the size of a fingernail
solders a card that lies on purpose,
that studies the jet's mistakes
until it can say them back, word for word,
and the plane hears its own accent,
believes it,
and takes off.
