**FIRST ANNUAL JIMI HALLOWEEN COSTUME CONTEST**
**4th Floor Kitchen, 5:30 p.m. until the pizza runs out**
**Official Scoresheet. Head Judge: Dana (Facilities)**
**Other judges: Priyesh (Platform), Okonkwo (Legal, recused from the snack table for reasons that are his own)**

## Rules, as adopted by the Rubric Committee

1. In the jimi tradition, costumes depict an ordinary moment, not a monster. No witches. No zombies. Last year somebody came as "AI" in a silver bodysuit and we are still recovering.
2. Every costume is submitted with a caption. The caption does the explaining.
3. A costume that needs no explaining is not a costume. It is a person standing in a kitchen. Score it zero on Needs Explaining and skip The Click.
4. Judges verify that each costume matches its caption.
5. Judges do not ask contestants to explain themselves. The committee debated this for most of a lunch and agreed that checking is enough, takes less time, and is less awkward at a party.

**Scoring columns:** Mundanity (0–10). Needs Explaining (0–10, higher is better: you should have no idea what you're looking at until you read the caption). The Click (0–10: the small "oh, *yes*" when you read it).

*Head judge's margin note on Mundanity:* The best ordinary things are the ones you could stand inside all night without noticing. I read this week that you could stand on the floor of the biggest canyon in the solar system and think the ground was slightly uneven. That's the standard I'm holding people to.

## Entry No. 1

**Contestant:** Marguerite (Payments)
**Caption:** "Someone asking, during the outage, whether this is a good time to discuss the 1985 decision."

**Costume:** A laptop open to a status page that says *Investigating*. A printed slide titled FORTY YEARS. A polite, hopeful face.

**Mundanity:** 9. **Needs Explaining:** 7. **The Click:** 10.

*Notes:* She refreshed the status page all through judging. Around the halfway mark Priyesh checked his phone and said the outage was real and pull requests really were down, so we can't say for sure whether this was a costume or a Tuesday. Rule 4 says the costume has to match the caption, and it matched it better than we'd have liked. She said she has raised the 1985 decision at four outages so far and was told every time that it wasn't a good time. I gave a bonus point for persistence and took it back for realism.

## Entry No. 2

**Contestant:** Wendy (Storage)
**Caption:** "The person who knew first."

**Costume:** A cardigan and a notebook. That's all.

**Mundanity:** 10. **Needs Explaining:** 10. **The Click:** *withheld pending attribution.*

*Notes:* Under Rule 5 we didn't ask. She told us anyway, for eleven minutes, about an allocator from 1973 whose free function took the size of what you were freeing, "as it should," and how everyone forgot it and spent the next fifty years having the allocator remember it for them. Priyesh says she's right. I believe Priyesh. Neither of us could explain it back to her, so we confirmed she was right without knowing anything, which the rubric allows.

Inside the notebook cover is a pencil sketch of a twisted ladder, dated last spring. Two contestants from another floor have since come over to say the spiral was their idea. They are not wearing cardigans. They have a paper.

## Entry No. 3

**Contestant:** Ben (QA)
**Caption:** "Someone on the train trying not to laugh because the murder suspect said 'honestly' twice."

**Costume:** One arm up, holding an invisible strap. Phone held close to the face. Shoulders shaking.

**Mundanity:** 8. **Needs Explaining:** 9. **The Click:** 9.

*Notes:* The suspect is a chatbot in a detective game. Ben says it lies about some things that are unrelated to the case, and that this is "on purpose, for realism." Okonkwo said that's just witnesses. A strong entry. Ben has never once been on a train.

## Entry No. 4

**Contestant:** Theo (Site Reliability)
**Caption:** "Someone whose unauthorized fix has just been made permanent."

**Costume:** Hi-vis vest. A traffic cone with a pull request number written on it in Sharpie. Slightly dazed.

**Mundanity:** 7. **Needs Explaining:** 8. **The Click:** 8.

*Notes:* He put the cone down without asking anyone, on the grounds that people kept tripping in that spot. Okonkwo asked whether the cone had been approved. It had not been. Partway through the evening it was. Theo is now its owner and gets paged when it falls over. Several people who had walked past that spot for years without noticing anything wrong said they felt safer.

## Entries No. 5 through 10

**Contestants:** Sales
**Caption:** "Six people waiting for the microwave."

*Notes:* Scored as one entry, because that is what it was. Mundanity 10. Needs Explaining 0. Under Rule 3 this is just Sales standing in a kitchen, which they would have been doing anyway. Disqualified without prejudice. The popcorn was very good.

## Entry No. 11

**Contestant:** Jordan (Talent)
**Caption:** "A recruiter."

**Costume:** Quarter-zip. Lanyard. A warm, two-handed handshake.

**Mundanity:** 10. **Needs Explaining:** 0. **The Click:** n/a under Rule 3.

*Notes:*

Matches caption. Verified. (Rule 4.)

Showed each judge photos on its phone: two kids on a beach, a birthday cake, a very good dog. The dog had a name. I've forgotten it, and it was a lovely name.

Asked the judges only one thing, whether we were "open to new opportunities." Priyesh said he wasn't looking. Jordan said nobody is ever looking, and that's the fun of it.

Jordan's CEO joined by video call partway through to "say hi to the team." I didn't catch the company's name. It sounded like a company.

Gave each judge a short take-home: "a small repo, nothing heavy, an evening at most, just so we can see how you think." Priyesh ran his during the pizza break on the scoring laptop, because that's the one plugged into the projector. The scoring laptop also has admin on the kitchen display, the badge printer, and a shortcut called `deploy-wallet-prod`, which I assumed was for expenses.

Rule 5: did not ask the contestant to explain itself. Rule 3: needed no explaining. So under our rubric this entry can't score.

Under our rubric, it didn't need to.

## Final tally

**Winner on points:** Entry No. 2, pending attribution.

**Trophy:** not on its shelf. The shelf is fine.

**Kitchen display:** has said *Investigating* since 7:40 p.m. Marguerite says it's not hers.

**Scoring laptop:** works normally. The shell history has several commands, run during the pizza break, that none of the judges remember typing. The laptop was set up during the event.

**Jordan (Talent):** not in the company directory. We don't have a Talent department. Jordan's profile has over five hundred connections, including all three judges, who each accepted at roughly the same time this evening.

**Recommendation to next year's Rubric Committee:** add a column. I've tried several names for it and every one of them needs explaining.
