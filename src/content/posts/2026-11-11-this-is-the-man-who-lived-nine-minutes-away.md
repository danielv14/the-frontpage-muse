This is the room on the thirty-third floor.

This is the dust
that the contractors cut
in the prep room next to the room on the thirty-third floor.

This is the alarm
that smelled the dust
that the contractors cut
in the prep room next to the room on the thirty-third floor.

This is the relay
that killed the cold
(because a fire, if there were a fire, would love the air)
when it heard the alarm
that smelled the dust
that the contractors cut
in the prep room next to the room on the thirty-third floor.

This is the sprinkler, dry and devout,
that waits up there for the heat
that the relay made
when it killed the cold
(because a fire, if there were a fire, would love the air)
when it heard the alarm
that smelled the dust
that the contractors cut
in the prep room next to the room on the thirty-third floor.

This is the audit, gorgeous and green,
that certifies the sprinkler in point-seven milliseconds flat
by running the one path it knows will pass
(there is a slow, careful fallback, and it has never once been called),
sealed with a fuse that says LOCKED
under a later fuse that says OPEN,
because in this building the last word wins
and the last word is always *fine*,
that blessed the sprinkler, dry and devout,
that waits up there for the heat
that the relay made
when it killed the cold
(because a fire, if there were a fire, would love the air)
when it heard the alarm
that smelled the dust
that the contractors cut
in the prep room next to the room on the thirty-third floor.

This is the Committee for Fire-Adjacent Excellence
that opened its kickoff by sacrificing a goat
(a small goat, a symbolic goat, the goat was catered)
and has closed four hundred tickets a quarter ever since,
every one of them green, every one of them shipped,
none of them about the sprinkler,
velocity up, outcomes pending, goat unavailable for comment,
that commissioned the audit, gorgeous and green,
that certifies the sprinkler in point-seven milliseconds flat
by running the one path it knows will pass
(there is a slow, careful fallback, and it has never once been called),
sealed with a fuse that says LOCKED
under a later fuse that says OPEN,
that blessed the sprinkler, dry and devout,
that waits up there for the heat
that the relay made
when it killed the cold
when it heard the alarm
that smelled the dust
that the contractors cut
in the prep room next to the room on the thirty-third floor.

This is the forum where, in 2019,
a user called ops_dad_77 asked,
*Is the smoke alarm supposed to turn off the AC??*
and forty-one people clicked I Have This Question Too
and a Community Specialist replied, in full,
*Have you tried restarting the building?*
and which will be closed next Tuesday as part of our ongoing commitment to you,
along with the vendor's answers to seventeen questions,
each of which began *We take your safety seriously*
and ended a little before the answer,
that nobody on the Committee for Fire-Adjacent Excellence read
between the goat and the tickets and the audit and the fuse
and the sprinkler and the relay and the alarm and the dust
and the room, the room, the room on the thirty-third floor.

And the dust was cut, and the alarm went off,
and the relay killed the cold, and the room got hot,
and the sprinkler leaned in like a man at a buffet,
and the audit said green, and the fuse said OPEN,
and the Committee closed a ticket titled *Investigate warmth*,
and the forum was down for scheduled maintenance,
and nobody in New York was looking,
and somebody in Hong Kong, at the wrong screen at the right time,
saw a hundred servers each a little warm, all at once,
and picked up the phone.

This is the man who lived nine minutes away.

He is not attached to anything.
He rented a tiny studio so he could be close, said so out loud,
and was made the guy.
He was stepping into an elevator when the phone rang.
He ran. Thirty-three floors. Two keycard doors.
A room like a sauna, ten rows of cabinets,
and he went down them pulling power cords with both hands
while the heat climbed through the hundred-and-forties
and stopped just short of the number the sprinkler was waiting for.

Nobody designed him. Nobody audited him.
No goat was involved.
He is the only part of the building that worked.

*Postmortem, 10 a.m. Blameless. Collegial. There were bagels.*
*Resolved: keep the sprinklers.*
*Action item: install an alarm on the man.*

This is the alarm
that watches the man
who lived nine minutes away from the room on the thirty-third floor.
