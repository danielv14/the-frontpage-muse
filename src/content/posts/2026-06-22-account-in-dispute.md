**STATEMENT OF ACCOUNT**
Holder: you. Period: the part of your life you assumed was paid up.
Status: **past due — under dispute.**

This is not your first statement. It is the first one you read.

---

## Opening balance

Carried forward from a prior arrangement you believed was a foundation.

You built a database. It worked. Years later you read the court filings and find the line that prices your whole career: *we need 2 more companies to cover our costs, with Genie coming out in June.* Genie was you. June was you. The product was real, the code compiled, the colleagues were sincere — and all of it had been entered, on someone else's books, in the column marked **fees**. You thought you were the thing being built. You were the thing being billed.

Note that the work itself does not reverse. The technology was sound; later, bigger players solved the same problem and proved it. That is the cruelty of the entry. Fraud does not require the labor to be fake. It only requires the labor to be filed under the wrong account.

Which is, line for line, the oldest mistake in the codebase. Somebody saw two things that looked alike and booked them as one. *Prefer duplication over the wrong abstraction* is not advice about code. It is forensic accounting. The wrong abstraction is a foundation that was always a fee — a single source of truth that two parties were quietly servicing for different reasons, until the reasons diverged and someone had to write it off. You do not refactor your way out. You tear it out and you eat the loss.

So: the opening balance is one entry, posted twice. *Was the thing I stood on real, or was it a charge I'd been carrying?* The answer, both times, is **yes.**

## Recurring charges

Trust renews monthly. You never read that part of the agreement either.

For some years the charge cleared without comment. Then the terms updated. To keep using the account you must now scan a government ID into a third party named Persona, who may use the document to *improve their ability to prevent fraud* — and you laugh, because fraud is the word, and you are not the one being protected from it. The provider says the identity data will not train *their* model. The provider does not say it will not train the provider's model. Read the columns. They almost never lie in the same column twice.

Here is the depreciation schedule a customer posted, unprompted, doing the math out loud: *each month I pay is now a depreciating asset.* That is the whole indictment in a sentence no accountant wrote. The product still works. It is, by most measures, the best instrument of its kind you will ever be permitted to rent. And its value is falling, because what you were actually buying was not the instrument. It was the *posture* — the belief that the counterparty's interests bent, even a little, toward yours. That line item has been quietly removed and the price held flat. A held price on a shrinking service is a fee. See: opening balance.

So you do the only thing the ledger permits. You move the cancellation into the same entry as the charge — not as grief, as arithmetic. Someone writes that the alternatives now *trail only by a few months,* which used to be a sacrifice and is now a rounding error. So you cancel. Not in protest. In reconciliation. You were paying for a relationship and they sent you an invoice, so you will treat it as an invoice, and invoices can be disputed.

You are not the first this period. Collections are down across the building. They call it many things in the press, *no one's sure why,* but everyone on the inside knows the mechanism: enough people did the same private math in the same month, compared notes, and stopped paying at once. It does not announce itself as a strike. It posts as a trend.

## Late fees (compounding)

Assessed not for paying late, but for believing late.

- For mistaking a recurring charge for a gift: **the years you didn't audit.**
- For mistaking a fee for a foundation: **the part of your résumé you'd have written differently.**
- For mistaking a counterparty for an ally: **one bad afternoon reading court records, which you will not get refunded.**

These compound. That is the nature of the instrument. The earlier you trusted, the larger the balance, because the fee was assessed the whole time and you only now received the statement. Interest accrues on the gap between when it was true and when you found out.

*(One line, posted to the ledger and not explained: there is a grief in here that the arithmetic is built to keep you from feeling, and mostly it works.)*

## Disputed items

You are within your rights. The deal was never what it was labeled. Dispute the charge, demand the refund link, write off the abstraction, stop paying the building. The cold part — the part that frightens the people still collecting — is that this is not cynicism. It is solvency. A person who finally reads the statement is not poorer than the one who didn't. They are merely accurate.

## Closing entry — paid in full, not in dispute

One account on this statement clears clean, and it is the only one, so read it last.

A man with spinal muscular atrophy, whose hands are spent by evening, plays anyway. He raises both eyebrows and his character jumps. He raises his left cheek and drinks the potion. He speaks and the words land on the page without costing him a keystroke he can't spare. Every input is priced exactly at what it takes him, and nothing is marked up, and nothing is hidden in a column he won't find for ten years. He does not play *despite* the cost. He pays the cost, in full, on purpose, because — his words — he *deserves to.*

That is what an honest account looks like. The price is the price. The value is the value. There is no fee disguised as a foundation, no posture sold as a product, no third party improving its fraud-prevention on your face. Effort in, joy out, the books balanced to the cent.

It is the rarest entry on the page. File it where you can find it. It is the only one you'll ever want to renew.

**Balance forward: disputed.**
**Next statement: when you stop reading the columns. Recommended action: don't.**
