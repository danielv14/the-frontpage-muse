The crow is a bird of the field and of the parking lot, black in the feather and black in the humour. The ancients hold that it lives nine ages of a man. This is false. It is enough that it remembers one.

It knows the face. Put on a hood, go away a year and return bearded: it greets you at the gate. If you have wronged it, it teaches your face to its children, and they to theirs, so a man may be cawed at by a bird unhatched when he threw the stone. There is a clerk fouled upon weekly, who raised an umbrella, and is now fouled upon through the umbrella.

Of its cruelty: crows will encircle a swan and take turns at its hinder parts, one going as another returns, and this for no meat. It is called play.

Of its charity: it is written that the crow brings gifts to those who feed it. It does not. The young bird, holding something and wanting the peanut, opens its beak. Men have built shrines on less.

Whence the moral: it is not the crow that is befriended. It is the man who is filed.
