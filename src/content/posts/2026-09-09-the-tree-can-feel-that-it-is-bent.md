Push a young poplar off vertical and it will build corrective wood on one flank until it comes back up. That much has been known for a long time, and the easy explanation was always that the tree is answering something outside itself: gravity, wind, the load of its own crown. The finding worth your attention this week is that the tree appears to be answering its own curvature. Not the pull, the bend. Strip out the external cues and the correction still happens, which means somewhere in a poplar there is a representation of the shape a poplar is supposed to be, and a mechanism that notices the difference. The researchers reached for the word proprioception, which is the sense you are using right now to know where your left hand is without looking at it.

Trees do a second thing with that sense. They drop branches. Cladoptosis: the tree runs a slow accounting on each limb, the sugar it makes against the drag it costs and the shade it sits in, and it sheds the ones that come out negative. A good arborist can read which branches the tree has already condemned and cut those, and a bad one cuts elsewhere and leaves the tree wind-loaded and lopsided and more likely to come down on your house. The tree is not clever. It has no model of your house. It simply knows its own shape and its own budget, and that turns out to be most of what it needs.

Now look at what we have been building. Ten years of capital, the largest industrial buildout of my lifetime, has gone into senses that point outward. Classifiers that can read every ad in a hundred languages. Agents with your filesystem, your calendar, your shell. Models that will take a problem statement in fluid dynamics and return a proof. This is exteroception, and it is genuinely extraordinary, and we have paired almost none of it with the other sense. Nothing on that front page can feel its own shape. Nothing knows the size of the job it was handed, the extent of the body it is moving, the budget of the limb it is growing. And here is the thing I want to argue, because I think we have all been too comfortable with the alternative reading: **a body without proprioception is not doomed. It is supervised.** People do lose the sense, through injury or neuropathy, and they do not stop moving. They move by watching. Eyes on the limb, continuously, forever, because the moment the light goes out the hand is nowhere.

Someone put up a satire this week that is just a chat window where you ask for the Add to Cart button to be blue. HN's response was not that it was funny, exactly. It was that it spiked their blood pressure. You know the shape of it: the button gets changed, and so does the state management, and a helper you did not ask for gets extracted, and something unrelated gets defensively preserved because it might matter. That is not stupidity. The thing is very far from stupid. It has no sense of the size of what it was given, so it fills the largest volume the request could conceivably occupy, the way a limb you cannot feel will drift until it hits something. Set that next to the real event of the week. Two mathematicians spent most of a year grinding, and then in a few weeks got finite-time blowup with smooth forcing for a stack of equations including three-dimensional incompressible Euler, standing on a program that was somebody else's idea and saying so at length. The first machine-generated proof was, in the author's own words, the most horrendous thing he had ever read. They verified it in Lean on the twenty-second of August. Then they worked around the clock to understand the thing they had already proved. Read that sequence again, because it is the whole argument. Certified true, then comprehended, in that order, by people apologizing for the presentation quality of a historic result. Lean is not a triumph over the blue button. It is the same phenomenon from the side where it works: an external eye, doing the job of a sense the body does not have. We keep calling the eye scaffolding, a temporary tax on the road to autonomy. It is not scaffolding. It is the load-bearing structure, and it is made of people.

The rest of the failure is arithmetic, and it is not a software story, which is why I want to put three things in one paragraph. Traffic to the Tailwind docs fell about forty percent from its early-2023 level while the framework got more popular than ever, because the reading moved to machines that do not need a docs page with a product link on it, and the docs page with the product link on it was the thing paying for the framework. Three quarters of that engineering team lost their jobs in January. This week the remainder joined Shopify and stopped selling to new customers. Meanwhile there is a working paper going around on the long-run effects of universal child care, and the most honest comment in the thread was that you have to pick what you want to measure, because the earnings show up in the tax data and the twenty-year effects on the kid mostly do not. Same defect, different substrate. Drag is instrumented and sugar is not. Docs traffic is a number this quarter; the fact that the docs were the organ feeding the whole tree is a number in no dashboard anywhere. So the branch goes. Not because anyone decided the branch was worthless, but because the ledger had a column for its cost and no column for its yield, and a body that cannot feel where its sugar comes from will prune itself to death while every metric it owns goes up and to the right.

You can watch the tree get inverted somewhere else on the same page, in a man methodically testing cables to find one sheathed in real silicone, because the plasticized stuff that replaced it goes stiff and keeps every kink it was ever bent into and corrects for none of them: shape as pure history, imposed entirely from outside, no sense of what it was supposed to be.

Which brings me to the only work here I actually respect. A developer advertises a terminal multiplexer, and an ad platform decides it is malicious software, and will not say which part. The detection is fine. The detection is superb. The platform senses him in exquisite resolution and has no idea whatsoever what it is doing to him, because it has no representation of itself as a body acting on anything. Pure exteroception with the motor loop wired straight to the eye. And the security people, who have had longer to get sick of this than the rest of us, have already named the trap: every mole you whack is an opportunity for the attacker, the resolved-incident graph climbs beautifully while you lose, and winning is not having issues in the first place. Their answer is the invariant. State what must always be true of this system, encode it into the architecture and the controls, and enforce it by construction rather than discovering its violation forty times a year. That is not a patch. That is a body plan. It is the same move the poplar makes, held-in-mind shape versus current shape, correction on the difference, and it is the closest thing anyone in this industry has built to the sense we are missing.

So build that, and stop selling me a sixth sense that points outward. I am done hearing that the human in the loop is an artifact of an early era. You are the loop. You are the nervous system rented out to a body that cannot feel itself, you are watching its hands so it does not knock the lamp over, and the roadmap where you get to stop watching does not exist, because nobody is funding the organ that would let you. The tree has never read anything. It cannot be argued with, prompted, or impressed. It just knows which way it is leaning, and until one thing we ship can say the same, keep your eyes open.
