*BOUNCE EXCHANGE, Nov 27 (Muse Wire)*: The ball fell on Friday. Left settled at 1.00 and Right at zero after a volatile session, as traders weighed storage costs, aviation risk and thin holiday volume on the busiest shopping day of the year.

The exchange simulates each drop in full on its server before streaming it to traders on a short delay. Friday's drop, including the landing, was therefore complete before the first order was placed. The exchange confirmed that the result was "known to the server," and that the server was not taking questions.

## The session

Left opened soft at 0.19. Desks blamed storage, where a refurbished 18-terabyte drive, under four hundred Australian dollars two summers ago, was offered Friday at more than eleven hundred beneath a banner reading BLACK FRIDAY SPECIAL. Left rallied on the first bounce, which analysts attributed to reports that a Norwegian long-haul carrier had suspended all flights and might not resume them. Left fell on the second bounce, also on the airline. The third bounce, a hard left off a peg, was attributed to hard drives *and* the airline. One strategist described that combination as "a perfect storm," and another as "priced in."

Asked what an airline had to do with a ball, a strategist said the airline had not hedged its fuel. Asked what that had to do with a ball, she said the ball had not hedged either. Asked whether a ball can hedge, she called it "a fair point, and frankly bearish." Asked why drives were up, a second analyst said demand was strong, supply was consolidated, and the ball was falling, "which is never good for storage."

Volume was light. One participant said he had bought his drives in 2023 and had been in a good mood ever since.

*Other markets: The exchange's market maker, which takes no view on the ball, finished up. Nothing, a contrarian instrument, could not be quoted.*

## Algorithmic activity

Automated traders were active early and withdrew, as the rules require, once a sixth human arrived. One bot bought every share under five cents so fast that the price moved before any human click landed. A trader who had lost steadily all morning said he had responded by buying high, "which the bot turns into buying low by the time my order arrives." He was up four points at midday and lost them in the afternoon. Asked whether he had considered the ball, he said he had not seen it because the stream was buffered, and that in any case it had already landed.

New arrivals continued to receive ten free points, and most leave before spending them. Economists said this steadily raises the money supply per participant and is either inflationary or the business model.

*Other markets: The market maker was up. A third party active only on small screens was present at checkout all session, recording every card typed into the order form, whether or not the order filled. It took no position on Left or Right and reported strong results. Nothing was not quoted; its sponsors said it trades "by not participating," a type of order the exchange cannot record.*

## The fundamentals

Asked whether a deterministic drop made the outcome knowable, an exchange spokesman said yes. Asked whether anyone knew it, he said no. Asked how that differed from not knowing, he said it was "far more rigorous."

*Other markets: The market maker was up. The checkout party was up. A Florida firm that sells clients forecasts of the landing for a diagnostic fee, and says it never deals with the ball directly, was found to have privately asked the ball where it was going, talked it down to half, and billed clients the full figure plus the fee. Its website was unavailable by afternoon. Legal analysts said the arrangement followed long-standing maritime practice: under the right of wreck, whatever washes up belongs to whoever holds the shore. Under "no cure, no pay," the salvor bills only when something is recovered, and something is always recovered, if only the fee. The airline's aircraft will return to their lessor, which also leased them to the previous Norwegian carrier that collapsed. The lessor was up. Nothing was not quoted. One holder conceded she had bought sushi.*
