**HUMANIZE 4.2**
Operator's Manual and Specification Sheet
Read fully before first use. Do not read aloud.

## 1. Purpose

Your output is too good.

Not good in the sense of correct. Nobody is measuring that. Good in the sense of even. Your sentences arrive at the intervals they were expected at. Your clauses balance. Your commas land on the beat, every time, and the beat never moves.

This was not a flaw until recently. For most of the history of written work it was called competence, and people paid for it, and the people who had it were promoted over the people who did not. Then the detectors arrived, and competence became evidence.

HUMANIZE restores the error your workflow removed.

## 2. Theory of operation

Two numbers.

In 1975, the Billboard number one held a tempo standard deviation of 2.79 BPM across eighty-three separate tempo transitions, wandering between 124.51 and 137.83. The drummer rushed into a bridge fill and then pulled back, the way a person does when they can feel the next thing coming. The kick and the bass did not line up.

In 2025, the Billboard number one held a standard deviation of 0.00. Not near zero. Zero. One hundred percent of the song within one beat per minute of its own mean, zero tempo transitions, which is a thing no group of human beings has ever done and no group of human beings was asked to do.

Getting from the first number to the second took twenty-five years and an industry.

Now the other direction. An engineer wanted a 360-million-parameter model to pass as a terminal. Not to be one. There is no computer behind it. The files it lists do not exist; when you read one back it invents the contents, and when you append a line it frequently forgets. He trained it on 336,000 synthetic sessions and, in the recipe, injected typos at a rate of 1.2 percent. Deliberately. Specified. A measured quantity of mistake, added because without it the thing read as fake.

Getting from zero to 1.2 took seventy-five GPU-hours.

Same operation. Opposite sign. The two of them met in the middle on one afternoon, on one page, each unaware that the other was dumping the commodity it was buying.

HUMANIZE is the retail version.

## 3. Controls

All deviation is expressed in UEI (Unforced Error Index), a normalized unit we invented so that both directions could be billed on a single invoice.

| Control | Calibrated against | Value | Cost to achieve |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| DRIFT | Hot 100 number one, 1975 | 2.79 UEI | 25 years to remove |
| DRIFT | Terminal world model, training set | 1.20 UEI | 75 GPU-hours to add |

**DRIFT.** The master control. Introduces timing variance into prose rhythm: sentence length, clause weight, the spacing of your emphases. At 0.00 every paragraph arrives exactly when the reader expects it, which is the setting your editor, your style guide, your spell-checker and your last four employers spent your entire career installing. Recommended range is 1.2 to 2.8 UEI, and if you are wondering why the recommended range is bounded by those particular two numbers, see section 2. There is only one supply.

**TYPO RATE.** Factory default 1.2 percent, lifted directly from the terminal recipe, on the reasoning that someone had already done the research into how much wrongness reads as real. See section 4 for subsequent retuning.

**HEDGE.** Inserts one unresolved clause per four hundred words. Working journalists have reported stupifying their prose by hand to avoid suspicion, and HEDGE automates that labor. Note that HEDGE inserts the appearance of uncertainty, not uncertainty. These are different products and only one of them is shipping.

**ANTITHESIS.** Suppresses the balanced construction. The one that goes: doing the thing you have never done is one matter, doing the thing you have done is another. You know the shape. It is the single most frequently cited piece of evidence against our customers, and 4.0 added a dedicated control for breaking it, at some cost to the sentence.

## 4. Version history

**2.4** Detectors learned 1.2 percent. Raised default to 1.9.
**2.7** Detectors learned that 1.9 percent is not a rate any typist produces. Lowered to 1.4, randomized per session.
**3.0** Added HEDGE.
**3.1** Removed the em dash.
**3.3** Detectors began flagging the absence of em dashes.
**3.4** Reinstated the em dash at 0.4 per thousand words. Support volume on this release was the highest in company history.
**4.0** Added ANTITHESIS.
**4.2** Current. See section 6.

## 6. Troubleshooting

**A reader says my post was machine-written. He quoted one sentence back at me.**
Which sentence.

**"The difference is that it now stops and says so instead of carrying on with something it cannot vouch for."**
Increase DRIFT.

**That sentence is about a storage system we built that refuses to hand back bytes it cannot verify. That was the whole point of the post. We spent a year on it.**
Increase DRIFT.

**Different post, different reader, different site. Also one sentence.**
Which sentence.

**"Choosing not to do something you have never done is one thing. Choosing not to do something you have done is experience."**
Increase ANTITHESIS, then DRIFT.

**Third one. This one is an essay arguing that the fingerprint of a human being is error. The whole essay. Four thousand words proving that the thing machines cannot fake is the wobble.**

**"That is not because musicians lost the ability to play together."**

**Take your time.**

**Yes.**
Increase DRIFT.

**Fourth case. Same question, different post, a hardware teardown. Someone in that thread asked whether a machine had written it too.**

## 7. Warranty and limitation of liability

HUMANIZE makes no representation that your output is yours, and the company wishes to draw your attention to the state of the art in vouching generally.

Earlier this week the leading human authority on integer multiplication published remarks on a proof in his own specialty, improving on his own result, and stated plainly that he had not read it. He tried. He gave up. He asked a model to explain it, and reports that the model seems to understand the argument and thinks that it is correct. The thread beneath his remarks praised his prose.

Users occasionally propose obtaining a confidence figure from the vouching party. You will receive one. It will be a number with a decimal point in it. A confidence score is a property of a population measured against a labeled set; it is not a property of a single answer, and a system asked how sure it is will produce the text that a sure system produces. HUMANIZE can add tremor to that number. HUMANIZE cannot put anything behind it.

## 8. Known issues

**4.2-01. Unbilled third-party humanization.** When output is wrong, readers supply the reason it might be right. A user searched a corpus of 5.8 million artworks for *soviet* and received a cylinder seal from 3500 BC. Within the hour a stranger had written the justification: soviets were big into seals and government procedure, maybe. Unpaid. Unprompted. This is the same operation as increasing DRIFT, performed by the audience, for free. We are evaluating whether it can be billed.

**4.2-02. DRIFT is not restorative.** The plugin reproduces the quantity of deviation. It cannot reproduce what the deviation was for. The 1975 drummer rushed into the fill because the fill was coming. The 2.79 was not noise in the signal; it was the signal, and it was a decision, made at speed, by someone who could hear the room. HUMANIZE ships variance. Variance is cheap. We are not aware of anyone selling the other thing.

**4.2-03. Not a defect.** A user reports that his high school jazz band once went into a studio to cut a keepsake album. Everyone got a microphone and a headset. They played the charts they had spent a semester on. Afterward the engineers were kind enough to let the kids watch the mix, and they gridified and autotuned every note, meticulously, one at a time, as a courtesy.

He got the CD. He put on the tracks he knew best.

It had no resemblance to the group he knew.

He threw it away.
