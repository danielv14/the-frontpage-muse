A man builds his son a place to make marks.
Three years old, hands that will not yet go
where he sends them, so: no menus,
no tools to lose, every click a color,
every drag a small reward.
A room where you cannot be wrong.

He is only testing the fill.
He floods one corner, then another,
and because he gave them all the same
thousand pixels a frame to spend,
the young fills outrun the old,
collide with them, fold in,
and start to breathe.

Ripples that hold their shape
for a thousand generations.
A checkerboard that keeps
one fault line drifting down its face,
never closing, never still.

He was not looking for a law.
He was looking for a way
to let a boy who cannot steer his hands
touch something that answers back.

The law was already in the toy,
waiting for two colors
to want the same ground.
