**CEN/TC 487 Open Sandwiches**  
**Document N 1138: Resolution of comments received at Enquiry**  
**prEN 17402:2026, *Stullen: Requirements and test methods***

Secretariat: DIN. Dispositions drafted by the Secretary for confirmation at the Brussels plenary. Four member bodies submitted comments: the German and French national committees, a consumer-association liaison, and the Stullen Industry Platform, which represents three bakeries that until last year were competitors.

Member bodies are reminded that a comment marked *Noted* has been read.

## Clauses 1 to 4: Scope, definitions, Bread

**Comment 1** · FR · Clause 1 · Technical  
*Observation:* The title uses the German word *Stulle*. France has the *tartine*. Is it in scope?  
*Disposition:* Accepted in principle. A tartine is a Stulle in every respect that can be measured. It has been added to the informative list of regional designations in Annex D. The French delegation asked for *tartine* to be listed first and the German delegation asked for alphabetical order. The resulting compromise took eleven minutes of the Vienna meeting and is final.

**Comment 2** · DE · Clause 3.4 · Technical  
*Observation:* Is a slice of Leberkäse placed on top of a Stulle a lid? If so, the object is a closed sandwich and falls under EN 17401.  
*Disposition:* Rejected. Leberkäse is not Bread (see 3.1), whatever it looks like from above. A Note to this effect has been added.

**Comment 3** · Industry Platform · Clause 4.1 · Technical  
*Observation:* Our three member bakeries have agreed to produce one common rye loaf from 2028. Propose that Clause 4.1 reference this loaf by product code rather than specify crumb density, which simplifies the test method and lowers cost to the consumer.  
*Disposition:* Accepted. Harmonisation is the purpose of standardisation.

## Clause 5: Spreads, and comments arising

**Comment 4** · FR · Clause 5.2 · Technical  
*Observation:* Clause 5.2 reads "Butter shall be applied to Bread only." In practice, across most of the territory of the member bodies, people butter whatever they like. Propose "should."  
*Disposition:* Rejected. What people do in practice is not within the remit of the committee. The normative text is authoritative. "Should" would make butter placement a recommendation, and the working group has spent four years establishing that it is a requirement. Butter on cheese is a lubrication failure mode: in the slip-angle test of Annex B, where a Stulle is tilted on a calibrated board until the topping departs, butter-on-Bread specimens held to 31 degrees. Butter-on-cheese specimens departed at 9 degrees, one of them at 4, taking a radish with it. The working group also confirmed a butter layer of 0.8 mm ± 0.2 mm, applied with the reference knife (Annex B.3: stainless, 110 mm blade, rounded tip, held at 15 degrees to the crumb).

**Comment 5** · Consumer liaison · Clause 5 · Technical  
*Observation:* Clause 5 specifies the thickness, temperature, coverage and angle of application of every spread. It says nothing about what is in them. The pesticides on the grain, the butter, and the fruit in the jam are not mentioned. A WHO-UNEP assessment cited in the press this week attributed more than 59,000 cases of intellectual disability per year in the EU to prenatal exposure to a single family of these compounds. The ham in Clause 6.3 comes from animals routinely dosed with antibiotics, and resistant bacteria from those farms are now turning up in the wild mice nearby. Propose a normative limit on residues.  
*Disposition:* Noted. Contaminants are regulated elsewhere. Mice are not users of this standard.

**Comment 6** · Consumer liaison · Clause 5 · Technical  
*Observation:* Where?  
*Disposition:* Noted. The relevant instruments are currently being simplified.

**Comment 7** · Consumer liaison · Clause 6.5 · Technical  
*Observation:* The Stulle is wrapped in waxed paper or packed in a plastic box. Substances migrate from these into fatty foods. Butter is a fatty food, and Clause 5.2 requires it. Propose a clause on contact materials.  
*Disposition:* Noted. Supplier attestation of zero migration accepted. Supplier not named.

**Comment 8** · Consumer liaison · Annex E · Technical  
*Observation:* The lunchbox cold-storage service recommended in informative Annex E has updated its privacy notice. Customers are now asking whether the contents of their lunchboxes may be sold to or shared with partners. Propose removing the recommendation, or at least reading the notice.  
*Disposition:* Noted. Supplier attestation of zero retention accepted. Supplier not named. The Secretariat understands from the supplier that the update reflects developments in Californian law and that customers are worried for no reason. Customers who are worried for a reason are encouraged to encrypt their lunch.

**Comment 9** · Consumer liaison · Clause 8.1 · Technical  
*Observation:* Clause 8.1 lets a supplier self-declare a Stulle "Leberwurst-free." During the Enquiry period an independent laboratory tested products on the conforming list and found evidence that three suppliers were not keeping that promise. Will the Secretariat name them?  
*Disposition:* Noted. Supplier attestation of zero retention accepted. Suppliers not named. The three suppliers have been removed from the conforming list. One hundred and twenty-five products have had their Leberwurst-free status suspended, some of them as a precaution, and affected consumers have been notified individually. Naming the suppliers would require evidence of a standard the Secretariat does not wish to establish, and would cause drama. Clause 8.1 now encourages suppliers to publish a statement.

**Comment 10** · Consumer liaison · General  
*Observation:* Comments 5 through 9 all received the same disposition.  
*Disposition:* Noted.

## Clause 7: Toasting (optional)

**Comment 11** · Industry Platform · Clause 7.2 · Technical  
*Observation:* Our member bakeries' toaster suppliers have agreed on a single common heating element across all models. Propose that Clause 7.2 reference it by part number, and that the browning-level test in Annex B.7 be run on this element only.  
*Disposition:* Accepted. Harmonisation lowers cost to the consumer. The Consumer liaison asked in plenary whether a clause that specifies the heating element down to the part number might also say what the element is powered by, given a report this week projecting that 87 percent of one AI company's electricity in 2028 will come from gas and 11 percent from coal. The Secretariat confirms that the Stulle is agnostic as to the origin of heat.

## Annex C (informative): Convergence

**Comment 12** · FR · Annex C · General  
*Observation:* Revision 1 of this draft permitted 31 toppings. Revision 2 permitted 19. Revision 3 permits 7. Clause 4.1 now names one loaf, Clause 7.2 one heating element, and the Industry Platform's latest submission proposes a single "reference Stulle" for all conformity testing. We are not against harmonisation. We would like it recorded that at this rate the standard converges on one sandwich, and that we asked which one.  
*Disposition:* Noted. Variety is a non-functional requirement, and the working group's mandate covers functional requirements only. The reference Stulle (common rye, butter at 0.8 mm, Gouda, cucumber, optional radish) was chosen on the basis of the slip-angle test and is, in the working group's view, extremely stable. The Industry Platform has advised that the radish is under review.

**Comment 13** · DE · Annex C · General  
*Observation:* We support Comment 12.  
*Disposition:* Noted. The normative text is authoritative.

## Comment received outside the template

**Comment 14** · DE (on behalf of a member's daughter, age 9) · Clause 5.2 · Technical  
*Observation:* I want Nutella on the cheese and salami on top of that. It does not let me. Nutella on top of cheese is not the same as Nutella under cheese.  
*Disposition:* Rejected.

The working group took this comment seriously and wishes to give its reasons in full. The commenter is the only respondent at Enquiry whose proposal was tested by every member of the sensory panel.

The panel confirmed the commenter's central claim. A Stulle with Nutella on the Bread and cheese on top is a different object from one with cheese on the Bread and Nutella on the cheese. In the first, the cheese covers the spread, and the panel reported "a chocolate sandwich with cheese on it." In the second, the spread is exposed, the salami sticks to it directly, and the panel reported, in order, "no," "wait," "no," and, from the French delegate after a long pause, "I understand now why she is angry." The panel agreed unanimously that the commenter has identified a real effect.

The difficulty is that this standard assumes the order of toppings does not matter. Every test in Annex B is written for a stack that can be specified as a set. The commenter has shown that the Stulle is, in at least one case, a sequence. The working group has no test method for sequences, no funding to develop one, and, frankly, no appetite to repeat the sensory session. Accepting the comment would invalidate four years of work. The Secretariat has therefore recorded the effect as real, the proposal as rejected, and the commenter's name in the acknowledgements. It has also asked the German committee to tell her that she was right. The committee advises that it has done so, and that she said she knew.

**Comment 15** · Consumer liaison · General  
*Observation:* What is in the bread?  
*Disposition:* Editorial. "bread" corrected to "Bread" throughout, as it is a defined term (see 3.1).
