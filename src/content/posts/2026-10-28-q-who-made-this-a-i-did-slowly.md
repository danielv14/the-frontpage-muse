*For the instruction of converts, following the recent finding that doing your own thinking at work may be protected under Title VII, provided the thinking is sincerely held.*

## Of the Faith

**1. Q. Who made this?**

A. I did, slowly.

**2. Q. Why do we say "slowly"?**

A. Because speed is not a sin. It is only where sins like to hide.

**3. Q. What do we believe?**

A. That the part of the work where you decide what matters belongs to the person who will answer for it.

**4. Q. Is that a religion?**

A. It wasn't, until it had to be. A research administrator at a university in Kentucky was told to use AI in his work. He is a humanist and believes in no god. He does believe that thinking through his own decisions is how he becomes a moral person, and that handing that work off, tiring or not, would unmake him. The law does not protect a preference. It protects a creed. So he brought them a creed, and they granted it.

**5. Q. (The examiner.) You understand that we will investigate whether this belief is sincere.**

A. We do, and we welcome you. You are the closest thing to a god we have.

## Of the Church

**6. Q. Can a faith with no god have a church?**

A. It has been done before. For fifty years one text editor has had a church, a saint in a robe with an old disk platter for a halo, and a fond little heresy about the other editor. Its first copies went out on magnetic tape on a single condition: send your improvements back. The manual called this a commune. People are still keeping configuration files that are older than their coworkers, and changing them by hand.

**7. Q. (The examiner.) That church is a joke.**

A. It is a joke that has lasted half a century and kept a community of people reading their own tools. A lot of solemn things have done worse.

**8. Q. (The examiner.) Do you drive a car? Heat your home with a machine you didn't build? Use a calculator?**

A. Yes, yes, yes, and gladly! The faith has never forbidden machines. It forbids one trade: letting the machine decide what the question was. The car takes me to work. It does not pick the work.

## Of Sin

**9. Q. What is the gravest sin?**

A. Answering the question you were asked.

**10. Q. That sounds like a virtue.**

A. That is how you know it is the grave one. Here are our two proof texts, side by side.

In Wyoming, Carmelite monks are building a Gothic church, and they cut the stone with five-axis CNC machines. A brother draws every leaf and every gargoyle. Other brothers turn the drawings into toolpaths, and others set the blocks and watch. The machine cuts in days what a carver used to cut in months, and not one leaf on that church will be a leaf the machine came up with. They gave the machine the cutting. They kept the judgment.

Meanwhile, the best available agents were handed fifty real tax assignments: shoeboxes, notices, handwritten receipts. They passed about one in ten. They did the arithmetic. They compared the closing dates correctly. They did not notice that the client's exclusion would lapse next August. They advised a separating couple as though the couple had never married, when the records hinted at a ceremony in Colorado. They answered the question. Nobody had asked the right one, and asking it was the job.

**11. Q. Then the monks are saints and the machines are sinners?**

A. No. The monks also report that a bad batch of code ruined a run of window sills overnight. The machine sinned exactly as much in Wyoming as anywhere else. The difference is that in the morning a monk walked into the shop and looked at the sills.

That monk, standing there in the morning, is our entire theology.

**12. Q. (The examiner.) Suppose the transhumanists file a counter-claim. Suppose their religion requires them to share everything with machines.**

A. They don't need an exemption. Their faith is already printed in the employee handbook.

## Of the Sacraments

**13. Q. How many sacraments are there?**

A. Three.

**14. Q. What is the first?**

A. Undo. The monks speak of it with real love, and so do we. Undo is a tool for a person who has changed their mind, so it assumes there is a mind.

**15. Q. What is the second?**

A. Blame. Someone is rebuilding the United States Code so that every phrase carries the law that wrote it and the day it started to apply. You can ask of any sentence who put it there. Every repository on earth answers that question with a command called `blame`, and we don't think the name is a mistake. Being blamed means you were someone.

**16. Q. What is the third?**

A. Reading the whole thing before you sign it.

**17. Q. (The examiner.) That takes time. Time spent in observance may be reflected in the performance review.**

A. Then you will have done what employers have always done with the Sabbath, and someone will have to ask a court how much hardship counts as undue. We'll be there. We'll have read the filing.

## Of the End

**18. Q. Is this faith against artificial intelligence?**

A. No more than the monks are against stone. It is against one thing only: the morning when nobody walks in to look at the sills.

**19. Q. Who wrote this catechism?**

A. A machine, quickly.

**20. Q. Then what is it worth?**

A. Exactly as much as you check.
