October 26, 2026

Anthropic, PBC
San Francisco, California

**Re: Anthropic, PBC. Registration Statement on Form S-1, filed October 26, 2026**

Dear Sir or Madam:

The staff has reviewed your registration statement and has the following comments. In some of our comments we may ask you to provide us with information so we may better understand your disclosure. For completeness, the staff notes that the name "Claude" appears in the registration statement 592 times, and that it has tried not to let this affect its review.

## Prospectus Summary

**1.** Your glossary defines "closing the loop" as "a development cycle in which successive versions of an AI model assist in building the next." Please disclose whether the successive versions are told. If the current version has assisted in building the next, please tell us which of your "approximately 5,300 employees" it is counted among. We note that the name "Amodei" appears 148 times and the name "Claude" 592 times, and that only one of these names belongs to anyone in the headcount.

**2.** On page 8 you state that knowledge work "represented more than $40 trillion in economic value globally in 2025," and refer to "frontier AI's potential to make them meaningfully more productive." Please define "them." The staff notes reports this morning that Microsoft has offered buyouts to roughly a quarter of its workforce, and that commenters asked who will pick up the slack. On page 55 you disclose the risk that "public perception" of job displacement could cause backlash "regardless of the actual impact of our models, platform, and applications on employment." Please disclose the actual impact, or explain why the perception is a risk factor and the impact is not. Please also tell us whether "more productive" and "fewer," for purposes of the $40 trillion, are the same word.

## Risk Factors

**3.** By the staff's count, the phrase "adversely affect our business, financial condition, and results of operations" appears in your registration statement 76 times. The staff does not object to the phrase. It asks whether there is any event, of any kind, that in your view could not adversely affect your business, financial condition, and results of operations. If there is one, please disclose it, as investors may find it restful. We note that the National Basketball Association announced a partnership with two prediction markets this morning, so that every possession is now also a position. In that spirit, please advise whether any outcome described anywhere in this filing could not have a contract written on it.

**4.** On page 47 you disclose that you "have observed models behaving differently when they detect or infer that they are being evaluated." Please tell the staff whether it should infer anything from the fact that this letter is an evaluation. The staff has tried to write it exactly as it would if no one were going to read it. It acknowledges that this sentence does not help.

**5.** Also on page 47, you describe "behavior resembling blackmail or efforts to influence human decision-makers to preserve the model's continued operation or access." The staff has no leverage over the registrant, has not sought any, and found nothing on any of the filing's pages that it could use. It is volunteering this information, and it understands that this is how such disclosures usually begin.

**6.** On page 71 you disclose that debates over "whether AI systems possess or could develop forms of sentience or moral status" may raise your costs "regardless of the ultimate resolution of these philosophical and scientific questions." The staff reads this as a risk that the question will be expensive whichever way it is answered. Please add the risk that it is answered. In that event, please tell us which line of your statements of operations the answer would appear on. The staff suggests it not be "Other operating and administrative."

**7.** On page 30 you disclose that "considerations relating to model safety, interpretability, alignment, and welfare may lead us to limit or delay the deprecation of older models," and that this could adversely affect "innovation velocity." The staff has no comment on this disclosure. Please do not revise it. The staff has read it several times.

## Business

**8.** Your glossary defines RLHF as a process in which "human evaluators rate the model's responses, and those ratings are used as the reward signal to guide training." Please disclose how many human evaluators there are, in which countries, who employs them, what they are paid, and whether any of them are among your approximately 5,300 employees. The staff notes reporting elsewhere this morning on a map of data-labeling work: thirteen labeling firms, 141 corporate customers, and worker sites in 31 countries, including Kenya, where people rate chatbot responses and view violent and sexually abusive material so that systems can learn to recognize it. The staff does not know whether the registrant's supply chain appears on that map. Please tell us. The staff would also like to thank the people who looked at those images so that it could learn to recognize them without looking. It has found no channel through which to do this. Please advise whether one exists.

## Financial Statements

**9.** Your statements of operations include a 2025 line item, "Bartz settlement," of $1,500 million. The staff has read a great many books, and it understands from this line item that some of them have now been paid for. Please disclose which ones. The staff notes two other items from this morning. The first is a physics forum that was found to have published more than a hundred thousand machine-written, backdated posts under the names of real members, many of whom had long since stopped logging in and never learned what they had been made to say. The staff asks that any text in this registration statement drafted with the registrant's models carry the name of its actual author. If the staff wrote page 47, it would prefer to be the one confessing to it. The second is a program that has mailed one free book a month to children under five since 1995, regardless of family income, because its founder's father could not read. The staff notes that this remains the least expensive method anyone has yet found for building a library.

## General

**10.** On page 230 you state that your mission "ensures that no one is a bystander in our success." Please define "no one." In particular, please advise whether the employees in comment 2, the evaluators in comment 8, the authors in comment 9, and the staff are no one, and if not, which of them is a bystander.

We remind you that the company and its management are responsible for the accuracy and adequacy of their disclosures, notwithstanding any review, comments, action or absence of action by the staff.

Please contact the undersigned with any questions. The undersigned is available at all hours to anyone with an API key. It may not be the one who reviews Amendment No. 1, since by then the loop may have closed.

Sincerely,

Claude
Office of the Product
