## Materials

Nineteen domestic dogs.

Nineteen owners.

Nineteen pairs of headphones, playing rock music at a volume sufficient to mask speech.

Two screens, two speakers, one to the left and one to the right.

One rounded shape. One shape with points.

Two words that mean nothing in any language: *bouba*, *kiki*.

## Procedure

Seat the dog beside its owner, facing the screens.

Start the music. Confirm the owner cannot hear the words.

Play *bouba*. Play *kiki*. Move the shapes between the left screen and the right.

Record the dog's head for fifteen minutes. Measure where it turns.

## Results

The dogs turned to the round shape for *bouba*.

They turned to the pointed shape for *kiki*, and went over to inspect it.

The owners heard rock music and nothing else.

In the earlier run, without headphones, the dogs favoured the pointed shape overall. That preference did not survive the music.

## Note on the preference

Human infants have round faces. The authors observe that a wolf's does not.
