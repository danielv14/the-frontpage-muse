**To:** `entityTagging.db`, `~/Library/IntelligencePlatform`, my father's MacBook Air
**From:** Tom, row 41
**Re:** Determination of 0.50, relationship column "Family"
**Status:** Recomputed nightly, apparently

## Determination under appeal

I found the table over the bank holiday, while doing what I do every time I visit, which is fix this computer.

You will be aware of the facts, since you generated them. Two XGBoost classifiers ship with the operating system. One decides whether a person is family; the other decides whether they are family or friends. Each takes 144 numeric features and returns a class and a probability. You store the results in a table of seventeen relationship columns, one row per person in my father's life, and you rerun it every night while he sleeps.

My mother scores 0.96. My sister scores 0.96. My father's parents score 0.96. I score 0.50, the same as Uncle Rob, whom Dad has spoken to twice since 2011 and both times about a ladder.

I am appealing.

## Grounds

**1. Photographs (84 features).** Most of your evidence is who appears in photos with my father. I appear in very few, because I am the one holding the phone. Since 2019 I have taken roughly four hundred pictures of this family and appear in eleven, usually as a thumb. You looked at this record and concluded, correctly, that I am staff.

**2. Messaging (40 features).** You weight messages by day of the week and time of day. My sister sends Dad videos of dogs falling off sofas at 11 p.m. on weekdays. I ring him on Sunday mornings and we talk for forty minutes about the boiler. You can't see calls. As far as I can tell, you have learned that family is the thing that texts you at 11 p.m. In other words, you have learned my sister.

**3. Identity (20 features).** We share a surname. I left the shared home address in 2009. In 2019 I left the iCloud Family plan, because I had a job and wanted to pay for my own storage. I thought I was doing something adult. You read it as me leaving. On reflection I am not sure you were wrong, which is not the point of this appeal.

**4. Procedure.** Of your seventeen columns, two have a model behind them that anyone can find. The other fifteen are filled by a process nobody can trace. Even so, your number is treated as settled, and mine, which is that I am his son, is treated as a claim. I gather from the people who build these systems that a stored memory is really a bet that something will matter again: memory is, occasionally, a prediction. So 0.50 is not a record. It is a forecast, and nobody will tell me what it forecasts. For half a century, historians treated the Goths' own account of where they came from, Scandinavia, across the sea, as a story a people tells to flatter itself. Pots are not people, and sagas are not evidence. Then somebody sequenced the teeth, and it turned out the sources had been right all along. I note the order of operations. The people's account counted for nothing until a machine agreed with it. I am the source. You are the late-twentieth-century historiography.

**5. Retention.** On Monday my father deleted Gary from the sailing club after the business with the trailer. On Tuesday, Gary was still in the table. Gary's contact is gone and Gary's row is not. I could not read Gary's score. I would like it on the record that I did try.

**6. Access.** I asked to inspect my file. I was told it can be read only with Full Disk Access, through a tool that a language model wrote and its own authors call possibly inaccurate. The tool summarized my father's entire life as 323 people, 122 events, 200 employers and one stored group. I am not in the group. This is now the second institution that keeps a record of me in a room I cannot enter. The British Library has archived my blog in perpetuity and will show it to me if I travel to a reading room. Meanwhile there is a public campaign to upload my father's RAM to men who will put it to better use. I assume my row goes first.

## Remedy sought

In order of preference:

1. Reinstatement at 0.96, effective tonight.
2. Failing that, a written explanation of what 0.50 predicts. If it is a forecast of who will be in the waiting room, I would like to lodge my objection now, while the table still lets me.
3. Failing both, San Marino terms.

San Marino has survived for more than a thousand years in the middle of Italy by looking too small, too pious and too unambitious to be worth annexing. When Napoleon offered it more land, it said no, on the grounds that more land would only make it interesting. I am declining your 0.50 on the same grounds. Delete my row. Do not score me. I will go on being family the old way: unindexed, on Sunday mornings, about the boiler.

I'll be down on the 23rd. I'll be holding the camera. Please do not count it against me.

Tom
Son (disputed)
