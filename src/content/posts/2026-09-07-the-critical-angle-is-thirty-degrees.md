**Fig. 1.** *Canis familiaris* at a vertical trunk. Impingement angle approximately 30 degrees. Splashback: none observed.

**Fig. 2.** High speed sequence, stream against flat porcelain at 90 degrees. Panels (a) through (d): contact, crown, ejection, arrival. Panel (d) shows the shoe.

**Fig. 3.** Marcel Duchamp, *La Fontaine*, 1917. Porcelain, signed R. Mutt. Installed today in a working restroom, the object would attract no comment, and would work.

**Fig. 4.** The invariant. Below thirty degrees the return flow drops by roughly 95 percent. The angle does not vary with the height of the fixture, the pressure of the stream, or the man.

**Fig. 5.** Isogonal solutions. (a) Cornucopia, a curve revolved about the vertical axis; lowest splashback of any geometry tested. (b) Nautilus, logarithmic spiral in horizontal projection, after the shell; lip serviceable from 140 to 430 mm, which is to say from a child to a tall adult. (b) is the recommendation, being easier to clean.

**Fig. 6.** Volume deposited daily on the floors of nonresidential restrooms in the United States: on the order of one million liters. No figure. The quantity has no useful graphical form.
