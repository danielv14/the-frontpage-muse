You are invited to dinner this Thursday at seven. This is a real invitation with a real day attached to it and not the other kind, the kind that ends in "we should do this sometime," which is a phrase that means no and has always meant no.

Seven means anything up to eight.

## The terms, so you can stop doing the arithmetic

I know what you are calculating, so let me take it off you one piece at a time.

The apartment is small. Mine too. Four people is a dinner party and the fifth sits on the arm of the couch, and in the entire history of the world this has never once ruined an evening. If you own a folding chair we have a plan.

You have been indoors since breakfast and the thought of another room is unbearable. Same. We can eat on the steps. Outside counts, the park counts, a blanket and a bag of crackers at six counts, and if that is the version you will actually show up to then propose it and I will bring the blanket.

There is the comparison problem, where your place is humble and other people's are not. Nobody who would think less of you over your furniture is going to be at this table, which is most of the point of the table.

And there is the etiquette knot, the one where you will not invite yourself over and I keep forgetting to invite you, so the two of us wait politely for each other for eleven years. I am cutting it from my end. Thursday. You may cut it from yours at any time and the answer will be yes.

Bring nothing. I mean that operationally and not as a courtesy. Bringing something is the step where most dinners quietly die, because it converts a Thursday into an errand, and an errand can be postponed in a way that showing up cannot.

There is already wine. It is not good wine and it cost almost nothing, and that last part deserves a sentence, because the sentence is most of the reason I am writing to you at all. Roughly half of California's wine grapes went into this harvest with nobody under contract to buy them. A quarter of the acreage that stood at the peak has been pulled out of the dirt or left to go feral, a third-generation grower described the job as raising a beautiful crop and then dropping it on the ground, and the trellises are coming down so the almonds can go in.

Nobody decided that. There was no meeting, no vote, no signature at the bottom of anything. What happened is that a very large number of people across a very long run of unremarkable Thursdays did not have anybody over and did not open anything, and the sum of all that not-opening moved down the supply chain at the speed of an invoice and arrived, about eighteen months later, as a bulldozer with a driver in it on a Tuesday morning in the Central Valley, tearing out wire that somebody's grandfather strung by hand. So the bottle I am going to put in front of you is not a figure of speech. It is the surviving end of something that is being demolished by consensus, and the consensus is made up entirely of evenings like the one you are about to not have.

## Why Thursday and not, in general, sometime

Because the two halves of this are not the same size, and that is the only part of this letter I would defend in an argument.

Delhi spent about twenty-five years dragging its electricity losses from over fifty percent down to five, which meant transformers swapped until the failure rate went from eleven percent to under one, bare wire replaced with insulated cable so nobody could hook into it, mechanical meters to digital meters to smart meters, a hundred thousand unresolved billing complaints worked through one complaint at a time, and women hired out of the low-income neighborhoods to go door to door and collect the money because the men in vans were never going to manage it. Twenty-five years for that, and meanwhile the highway over the San Juans still has no guardrail between the lane and the drop, and everyone who lives there knows, and there are people who will drive eight hours the long way round rather than take it, and the reason it has not been fixed is not that the problem is a secret. It is that not fixing it costs nobody anything this week, and fixing it has to become somebody's whole career.

That is the shape. Coming apart is distributed and free. Going back together is centralized, expensive, takes a quarter of a century, and requires one specific named person to decide to spend their life on it.

You cannot decide to have the vineyard back. That option is not on the table and will not be on the table in your lifetime. A vine takes years to come into fruit, the wire is already in a pile behind the shed, the almonds are in the ground.

You can decide to have three people over on Thursday. That one is genuinely available. It costs one evening and some tidying you had to do anyway. It is the cheapest item on the entire list and it is also the only item on the entire list, which is why I am being this insistent about a day of the week.

## Fine print

You are mostly doing this already, incidentally. You have just stopped calling it hosting. Somebody is out there right now rebuilding an uncut Leone out of a torrent and a laserdisc and handing it to strangers for nothing. Somebody is keeping a dead physicist's energy arithmetic numerically current in a git repo a decade after he died, patching in the solar prices he never lived to see so the book still works. Somebody posted a browser tank game on Monday and by Tuesday eleven people had walked in and told him the moon is too heavy, the moon is too fast, you can just drive up to your opponent and fire point blank, and he said good catch, fix in progress. Somebody has kept a server from 1993 alive for thirty-three years on an operating system nobody has touched since the early 2000s, which means that this year, somewhere, a stranger wrote code for that machine. Every one of them set a table and left the door open. None of them got paid. All of them got guests.

## RSVP

Reply yes or no. Not maybe. Maybe is where evenings go to die.

If Thursday is bad, name a different day. Naming a day is the entire skill. It is also the only genuinely difficult part of any of this, which should tell you something about how little the rest of it costs.

The house will be a mess. I am telling you in advance so that you do not have to perform surprise and I do not have to spend Thursday afternoon cleaning instead of cooking. The food will be fine. Not good. Fine.

Come anyway. Seven. Bring nothing.
