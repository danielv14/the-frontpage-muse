The captcha came first, which in hindsight was the whole story arriving early and being ignored.

Marek Sobota clicked the box that said he was not a robot. It thought about this for four seconds and then produced nine tiles of a crosswalk and asked him to select all squares containing a bicycle. There was a bicycle wheel bisected by the tile boundary. There always is. He guessed, guessed wrong, and got a fresh grid of traffic lights.

It was 21:40 on a Thursday. He had fourteen papers to review by Friday, for which he would be paid nothing, and he was on the fourth.

The fourth had six authors. Something in the list was wrong in the way a room is wrong when a chair has been moved: he could not have said what, only that the arrangement was off. Two of the names had the shape of names without the weight of them. He wrote them down on the back of an envelope, which is a thing he still did, and started with the fourth author, D. Karvonen.

D. Karvonen had an ORCID. It had been registered in March and contained nothing before March. The institutional page in the affiliation returned a 404, and the department's actual staff directory, which took three clicks and another captcha to reach, listed nineteen people, none of them Karvonen. The Scholar profile was six weeks old and carried four hundred citations, all of them from papers by the same nine-name cluster citing each other in a tight braid, the way a rumour does.

While he was doing this, the box on the shelf behind him was telling a server in Guangdong that it was a Samsung Galaxy in Ohio.

He did not know that yet. He knew only that his own connection had been flagged for the last three weeks, which was why the internet kept stopping to ask him whether he was a person. It asked him this eleven times that evening. It did not ask it once of D. Karvonen, who had a portrait: a woman in her forties, warm smile, slightly wrong ear. He put the portrait through reverse image search and found her on the website of a dental practice in Bratislava, where she has worked since 2019 and is, presumably, a real person with real patients and no idea that she has recently co-authored work on multi-temporal crop classification.

The second name, M. Alcorta, took twenty minutes and was worse, because M. Alcorta had a LinkedIn with eleven connections and an email at a domain registered to a privacy shield in Reykjavik.

He got up to make coffee and the machine was in the kitchen, which is how he finally looked at the box.

It was an H96, nineteen euros ninety, bought two Christmases ago for his mother, who could not work it and gave it back. It sat on the shelf under the router with a red LED. He had unplugged the HDMI months ago. The LED was blue.

You can find out what it is doing in about ten minutes, which he spent standing up with the coffee going cold. The box watches the HDMI line. When there is a signal, it behaves, more or less, and rents your connection out as a residential proxy to whoever pays. When the signal drops, when you have gone to bed or in his case gone to a conference deadline, it clocks in. It reports itself as one of four phone models. It opens news sites that nobody wrote, generated in bulk in a dozen categories, populated by writers who are also not people, and it clicks the advertisements on them. Thirty-eight thousand of these boxes, doing this. Fifty thousand dollars a day, moving out of real advertising budgets into a company whose contact address bounces because the inbox is full.

He stood there and did the arithmetic he was going to keep doing for the rest of the night. The box had been given a reason to be diligent. Someone had sat down and engineered an incentive into it, at the level of the HDMI pin, so that it would work while he slept and stop while he watched. Nobody had done that for him. His diligence had been requested by email.

He went back and finished the review. He wrote it properly: the ORCID dates, the dentist, the 404, the citation braid, screenshots, confidence 5. Then he came to the submission form, which wanted him to rate novelty on a ten-point scale, and significance on a ten-point scale, and clarity, and reproducibility, and offered a free-text box captioned "Additional comments to the Area Chair."

There was no field for it. Not a checkbox, not a flag, nothing in the taxonomy of the form that corresponded to author four is not a person. He put it in Additional comments. Somewhere around midnight his laptop, updating something, asked him to confirm his age range in order to continue, and he confirmed that he was over eighteen to a machine that has never once asked the box anything at all.

He had a tab open from that afternoon, a mailing list thread he had been reading out of idle interest. A compiler project had announced it would not accept contributions of any legally significant size that came out of a language model. The thread was four hundred comments of people accusing each other of hysteria and of collaboration, and underneath all of it the actual argument was three lines long and dry as a bone: the licence runs on copyright, copyright needs a human author, and if there is no author there is nobody to bind. They were not saying the code was bad. They were saying that a missing person is a hole in the load path.

That was the thing he could not get past. Somewhere in the world there was one committee for whom the absent human was structural, a beam you cannot remove, and it happened to be a group of people arguing about C. In his conference the absent human was a matter of etiquette. He had spent five hours performing a courtesy. The form told him so, politely, by not having anywhere to put it.

The notifications came eleven weeks later, on a Tuesday, at 08:14. Both papers accepted. Both accepted as orals, which is the top of the pile, twelve minutes on a stage in a large room. The meta-review said the reviewer's concerns regarding authorship were noted and considered outside the scope of technical evaluation. It read like it had been written by something with no stake in the sentence.

Underneath was a second email, warmly worded, inviting him to serve as Area Chair for the next cycle, with the good news that the AI-assisted review pilot would be expanded to help manage load.

He sat with the coffee, which was again cold, and looked at the shelf.

Fractions of a cent per click, thousands of times a night, is not much. But it is more than nothing, and nothing is the number he had agreed to, in writing, in a form with a ten-point scale for novelty.

"Congratulations to Dr. Karvonen," he said, to the empty apartment.

The apartment took it well. At 23:51 that night the LED went from red to blue, right on schedule, and the only thing in the room that had been paid to be thorough got back to work.
